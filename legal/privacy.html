Privacy Policy

Page & Plate · Last updated 28 September 2026

Page & Plate has no accounts and no sign-in. We do not know who you are, and there is nothing to log into. This page describes exactly what happens to what you put into the app.

What stays on your device

Your recipes, your photographs of cookbook pages, your week's plan and your settings are stored on your device and, if you have iCloud turned on, in your own private iCloud database — the same place your other apps' iCloud data lives. That is Apple's storage, tied to your Apple Account, and we have no access to it. It is not on our servers and we cannot read it, recover it, or hand it to anyone.

Deleting the app removes the copy on that device. Your iCloud copy stays until you delete it in iOS Settings, and comes back if you reinstall.

What leaves your device, and why

Reading a cookbook page

When you photograph a page and ask the app to read it, that image is sent to our server and passed to Anthropic's API, which reads the ingredients and returns them as text. This is the only time a photograph of yours leaves your device, and it only happens when you ask for it.

  • We do not store the image. It exists for the length of that one request and is then gone.
  • We do not store the ingredients that come back. They are returned to your device and saved there.
  • We never log the image or the text extracted from it.
  • A recipe you type in yourself never leaves your device at all — nothing is sent, because there is nothing to read.

What our server records

For each request our server writes one line to its log containing: the first eight characters of a randomly generated device identifier, how many images were in the request, how long it took, whether it succeeded, and how much of the allowance has been used. No image, no recipe, no ingredient, no title, and nothing that names you.

To count your free scans and the weekly allowance, our server keeps a small counter. It is stored against either a random identifier the app generates, or — on devices that support it — an anonymous key produced by Apple's App Attest, which confirms a request came from a genuine, unmodified copy of the app. Neither is linked to your name, your Apple Account or your email, and neither can be used to identify you.

Subscriptions

If you subscribe, Apple gives the app a signed receipt for that purchase. The app sends it to our server so we can check the subscription is real before allowing unlimited scanning. We do not receive your name, your email address or your payment details — Apple does not give them to us, and we do not want them. Payment is handled entirely by Apple.

Sharing a plan with your household

If you share your plan, that sharing runs through Apple's CloudKit, using your Apple Account and the Apple Accounts of the people you invite. It does not pass through our servers and we hold no copy of it.

The people you share with can see:

  • The week's plan — what is on which day, the portions, and any note left on a meal
  • Your recipes' titles, source, ingredients and a small thumbnail
  • The name you have chosen to be known by in that household

They cannot see your photographs of cookbook pages. Those are never shared, with anyone, under any circumstances. They also cannot see recipes you have not put into a household, your other households, or anything in your own plan outside that household.

A recipe belongs to whoever scanned or typed it. If you leave a household, or are removed from one, your recipes leave with you and the others lose access to them. If the person who runs a household stops sharing it — by choice, or because their subscription ends — everyone else loses access to that plan, and is told only that it is no longer shared. Nobody is told why.

The name you type for yourself is visible to the people in your households and to nobody else. It is not verified, and you can change or remove it at any time in Settings.

Reminders

When you send a shopping list to Apple Reminders, the app asks your permission first and then only adds reminders. It never reads, changes or deletes reminders that are already there. Your reminders stay on your device and in your iCloud; we never see them.

What we do not do

  • No advertising, no advertising identifiers, no ad networks.
  • No analytics or tracking SDKs. Nothing follows you between apps or websites.
  • We do not sell, rent or share your information with anyone, because we do not have it.
  • We do not build a profile of you, and we could not — there is no account to attach one to.

Children

Page & Plate is not aimed at children and collects nothing that would identify one. Anybody may be invited into a household by the person who runs it, and a household is visible only to the people in it.

Your rights

Under UK and EU data protection law you have the right to ask what personal data is held about you, and to have it corrected or erased. In practice we hold almost nothing: an anonymous scan counter with no name attached to it, and server logs that contain no personal data. If you would like the counter associated with your device removed, write to us and we will delete it.

Everything else is in your own iCloud, where you control it directly: delete a recipe in the app, or turn iCloud off for Page & Plate in iOS Settings.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what happens to your information, the date at the top will change and the new version will be published here before it takes effect.
