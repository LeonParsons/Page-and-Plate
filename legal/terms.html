What the app does

Page & Plate lets you photograph a page from a cookbook you own, reads the ingredient list from that photograph, scales the quantities to the number of portions you want, puts recipes on the days of a week, and sends the week's shopping to Apple Reminders. You can also type a recipe in yourself. With a subscription you can share a plan with other people, and everyone in it can edit the same week.

The app stores the ingredients and the source, not the method. The method stays in your book.

The free tier

Your first seven scans are free. A scan is one photographed recipe; a reading that fails does not count against you. The free scans are per device, for the life of that device, and do not renew.

Typing a recipe in is free and unlimited, on any tier, with or without a subscription.

What a subscription includes

A subscription covers everything you need to cook breakfast, lunch and dinner each week. To keep the service working for everyone, there is a fair-use limit on how much can be scanned in a short period; it is set well above ordinary household cooking, and if you ever reach it the app tells you when you can scan again. It is not intended to affect anybody cooking for their own home.

A subscription also lets you share a plan with your household. Being in somebody's household is free — only the person who runs it needs to subscribe.

If a subscription ends

If a payment fails, we do not end anything straight away: while Apple is retrying the payment, or during any grace period Apple gives, a shared plan keeps working and the person who runs it is told there is a problem.

When a subscription genuinely ends — it expires, or is cancelled or refunded — the plan stops being shared. Everyone else loses access to it and is told only that it is no longer shared. The person who ran it keeps the week: it becomes their own plan again, except for meals cooked from other people's recipes, which cannot come with it because those recipes belong to their authors. Nobody's own recipes are ever deleted.

Sharing runs through Apple's CloudKit, using your Apple Account. What the people you invite can and cannot see is set out in the Privacy Policy; in short, they see the week and your recipes' ingredients, and never your photographs of a page.

You are responsible for who you invite. Anyone in a household can add, change and remove meals, and can see the recipes everyone else has put in. You can remove someone, and they can leave, at any time.
